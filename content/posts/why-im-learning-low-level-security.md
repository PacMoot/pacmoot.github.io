## Where I'm Starting
I've been in the tech field for a couple of years now but have yet to find something that really clicked with me. Started off with tech support / sys admin type work then migrated to data analysis, yet still feel like something is missing. I know I want to do something with computers and technology but have yet to find the path that scratches that itch for me.

## Why Low-Level Security?
It's a bit of a funny story, but it began at my current job as a data analyst where my coworkers and I play a web game during our break to guess where various countries are located. I'm pretty bad at geography, so this led me to start sharpening my geography skills to maybe one day earn my coworkers' undying respect for my unrivaled map knowledge (Spoiler: There are *A LOT* more countries than I realized...). 

During my studies, I stumbled across a geography YouTuber named [Rainbolt](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oD10LyQvhq0&t=103s) who has a few prominent videos featuring something called OSINT (open-source intelligence). Doing more research into this "OSINT" after having it pique my interest, I discovered an organization called [Trace Labs](https://tracelabs.org/) that uses publicly available information to coordinate OSINT investigations into missing persons cases. I found this to be really interesting as who hasn't seen those crime or hacking TV shows (I'm looking at you, *Mr. Robot*) that feature similar skills and thought, "I'd be so much cooler if I knew how to do that." A very noble reason to learn any skill, I know. Alas, I digress.  

In the end, my adventure down this rabbit hole took me to the domain of cybersecurity as a whole and started to rekindle an interest in it I used to have back as a teenager. In this domain, a forked path lies ahead between two current interests of mine: vulnerability research and embedded systems security. Both of them tickled a certain part of my nerd brain when learning what they do. 

## The Plan

I'm sure you're thinking, "That's great and all, you suck at geography and that led you to find your true calling in life. *Who cares?*" But if you've gotten this far, then I can certainly say you care, random person on the internet (or potential employer [*I'll work really hard, I promise*]). 

And no, "low-level" doesn't mean easy mode. It means what runs under the apps you use every day, from the firmware to the physical processor and more hiding inside each device.

So, the plan. I have broken it up into two main parts. The first is learning the foundational skills that both paths (vulnerability research and embedded security) rely upon, such as:
- Linux
- C
- Assembly
- Reverse Engineering
- Basic Hardware Understanding
- And More!

I estimate that getting to a functional level will take around a year at 20-25 hours a week. After that comes the choice of focusing on either of the two paths in question, where I hope to have enough context from my studies to make an educated decision. 

## Why and How I'll Learn in Public
Now, why am I deciding to document my journey for the world to laugh at and judge, you might ask? Why, of course, to laugh at and judge myself in a year for how naive I was to think this would be a piece of cake! But primarily to document what I've learned and see just how far my knowledge and skills have truly progressed. 

The current post cadence will probably be ~once a week where I'll detail what I'm learning that week.
